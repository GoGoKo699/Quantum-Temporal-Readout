# Learning Quantum Temporal Readout

A detector can produce a fair bit on every individual read while preserving the desired Pauli expectation in its joint record. Start with a shared random sign on two successive reads, then follow how the same idea extends to a causal quantum detector and an unknown temporal correction rule.

| Route | What it explains |
|---|---|
| [One-qubit worked example](WORKED_EXAMPLE.md) | Track the ideal sign, the two corrected detector outputs, and the finite calibration certificate. |
| [Watrous bridge](WATROUS_BRIDGE.md) | Read the relevant textbook sections on states, measurements, and discrimination, then connect them to the temporal problem. |
| [Thirty milestones](MILESTONES.md) | Follow the conceptual dependencies from an ideal Pauli measurement to the full temporal and statistical resource account. |
| [Source-only worked comparison](../science/source-only-parity.md#encoding-and-estimation) | Compare identity and CNOT on two fresh source copies and track which signed moment each circuit places in the same corrected parity. |
| [Scientific account](../science/README.md) | Find the models, exact statements, proofs, and physical interpretation. |

The educational anchor is John Watrous's *The Theory of Quantum Information* (Cambridge University Press, 2018). The [bridge](WATROUS_BRIDGE.md) points to the relevant sections and identifies the steps supplied by the project.
