## Measurement twirling and calibrated shadows

Van den Berg, Minev, and Temme turn the bias of a fixed readout confusion matrix into a scalar response for each Pauli parity. Zero-state readouts estimate that response and yield a shot bound for the normalized estimator. The block twirl here uses the same group action on a product alphabet that includes time slots. For a fixed detector and one prescribed temporal parity, the resulting multiplicative identity already follows from that construction. [Van den Berg, Minev, and Temme, Protocols 1–2 and Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.09738).

Hashim et al. use full Pauli measurement randomized compiling to tailor a quantum measurement to stochastic bit errors, and use quasiprobabilistic mitigation for midcircuit measurement and adaptive feedback. The full masks used here have that same twirling role. Winick et al. extend randomized compiling to time-varying and non-Markovian gate errors under assumptions on the easy gates. Their theorems allow stochastic errors conditioned on the environment. The project uses retained temporal correlations to estimate a source observable by inserting destructive known-input references. [Hashim et al., Sections II–III](https://doi.org/10.1103/PRXQuantum.6.010307) · [Winick et al., Theorems 2–3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07500).

Chen et al. add a calibration phase to classical shadows and analyze sample efficiency with imperfect readout. Nguyen shows that bit-flip twirling suffices for shadow mitigation in a readout-error model with crosstalk. The product basis acquisition and support-wise zero-state calibration in this repository inherit these techniques. The score examined here uses the whole time block, including the reports generated by known references, so its informative contrast may be entirely absent from the one-shot measurement. [Chen et al.](https://doi.org/10.1103/PRXQuantum.2.030348) · [Nguyen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17328).

## Calibration through drift

Korhonen et al. intersperse detector tomography with state measurement to sample the same time-averaged detector, including on hardware with telegraph-like fluctuations. Wilkens et al. interleave acquisition batches with zero-state calibration and apply Pauli-$`X`$ twirls on both. These are precedents for scheduling references and data together. Here each block contains a reference probe whose report enters the score itself. A fresh private arm coin matches the two score means under the same history-dependent instrument. This yields a finite confidence set with no cross-block stationarity assumption and supports cases in which the average one-shot response is exactly zero. [Korhonen et al., Sections II.4 and IV.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.02575v2) · [Wilkens et al., Sections II–III](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28307v2).

Morales-Hashemi, Smith, and Cao compare fixed and redrawn signs for weak coherent rotations in repeated stabilizer memory circuits. Their models have equal syndrome statistics and the same multi-time Pauli-twirled description, yet can require different logical corrections. That work also asks which noise information merits calibration. The present readout example uses classical temporal dependence that remains visible after twirling, with Pauli expectation estimation as the task. [Morales-Hashemi, Smith, and Cao, Sections I and III](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03545).

## Differential references and the acquisition cost

Differential phase detection has long used a nearby symbol as a relative phase reference. Multiplying two signs to eliminate a common binary polarity is the corresponding elementary digital operation. The common-polarity example here illustrates the readout resource. [Differential phase-shift-keyed communication system, U.S. Patent 3,816,657](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19740018541).

A physical alternative uses a simultaneous reference tone and analogue processing. Adisa et al. recover transmon state separation under substantial carrier-phase drift using a Pound–Drever–Hall receiver. This is an important baseline for claims about hardware. The [thresholded example](../science/physical-model.md) prices a digital pilot as a separate detector call and gives a direct test with identical one-shot marginals and different joint contrasts. [Adisa et al., Fig. 3](https://doi.org/10.1103/ng1d-6gb8).

The central new statistical question is the price of discovering how to use those extra reference reports. The [unstructured context model](../science/context-cost.md) has fully recoverable source signs when its decoder is supplied. When the decoder is unknown, reference and signal blocks have to meet in the same contexts. A lookup estimator and a matching lower bound quantify that additional cost under a fixed budget. The [detector model](../science/model.md) gives exact examples at every block length in which all proper temporal marginals are uninformative.
